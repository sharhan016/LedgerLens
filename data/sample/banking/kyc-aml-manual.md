# Standard KYC evidence

For this fictional demonstration, an individual must provide one government-issued photo
identity document, one current address document issued within the previous three months,
a tax identifier, and a recent facial photograph. Staff must verify the identity number,
expiry date, name, and date of birth and record the evidence type without copying more
personal data than the procedure requires.

# Failed or incomplete verification

If KYC verification fails, the application must not be activated. For an existing account
whose periodic review fails, outgoing transfers, cash withdrawals, and new product
applications are restricted; incoming credits and regulator-mandated refunds remain
available. The customer receives a remediation notice and has 30 calendar days to provide
corrected evidence. Unresolved cases are escalated to Compliance for closure review.

# Suspicious activity

Staff must not disclose that a suspicious-activity review is underway. A monitoring alert
is not itself proof of wrongdoing. The analyst documents the observed pattern, compares it
with the customer's expected activity, and either closes the alert with rationale or sends
it to Compliance. Compliance alone may submit the fictional Suspicious Transaction Report,
normally within seven working days of determining that suspicion is substantiated.
